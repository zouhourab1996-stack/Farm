Water-Wise Farming in Tunisia: Working With a Dry Climate

In dry and semi-arid regions, productive land management begins with understanding rainfall, slope, soil and the limited water available through the year.

Protect the soil surface

Soil cover from suitable plants or retained crop residues can reduce direct exposure to sun and raindrop impact. The right cover depends on rainfall and crop needs: in water-limited settings, managers must avoid plant competition that could stress trees or crops.

Slow runoff where it is safe and appropriate

On sloping land, heavy rain can move soil downhill. Contour-aligned features, terraces and maintained stone structures may slow water and sediment movement when properly designed and maintained. Poorly designed barriers can redirect water and create erosion elsewhere, so local expertise matters.

Harvest rainwater thoughtfully

Rainwater harvesting systems should be matched to the catchment, slope, soil and expected rainfall. Their purpose is to make better use of seasonal runoff, not to promise water security in every drought.

Think beyond one field

Water, soil and biodiversity cross property boundaries. Research from Tunisia highlights the value of landscape-level cooperation between farmers and communities when addressing erosion, reservoirs, grazing and restoration.
